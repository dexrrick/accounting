# Direct Answer

For a small business handling unpaid supplier invoices, accrual accounting requires recognizing expenses and liabilities when goods or services are received regardless of when cash changes hands, whereas cash accounting records expenses only when cash is actually paid out to the supplier.

# Explanation

- Under the accrual basis of accounting, transactions are recorded in the periods in which the economic events occur.
- Under the cash basis of accounting, revenues and expenses are tracked strictly based on the physical movement of cash.
- Because the supplier invoice is unpaid, no cash outflow has occurred, meaning the expense is omitted from the income statement and the liability is omitted from the balance sheet until the settlement payment is eventually made.
- Choosing between these methods affects how financial performance is portrayed.
- Accrual accounting provides a more comprehensive view of obligations and financial commitments by matching expenses to the period the related benefits were consumed, whereas cash accounting offers simplicity and tracks immediate liquidity but may distort profitability when significant bills remain unpaid.

# Accounting Treatment

- Under accrual frameworks such as SFRS(I), the accrual basis is mandatory for general-purpose financial reporting to ensure that liabilities and matching expenses are captured in the correct accounting period.

# Conditions and Important Notes

- The applicability of either method depends on statutory reporting requirements, entity size, and whether the business prepares general-purpose financial statements or simpler tax-only computations.
