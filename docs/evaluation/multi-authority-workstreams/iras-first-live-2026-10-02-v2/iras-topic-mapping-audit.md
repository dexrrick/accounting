## Recognition boundaries

The 33-case regression uses bounded phrase families rather than the fixed sentences alone. Private-expense recognition requires the expense and an explicit corporate income-tax treatment or deductibility outcome to be linked in the same clause, or a later treatment clause to refer back to that expense. Anaphoric follow-ups such as “and is it deductible” preserve the link; a separate requested tax outcome does not attach to an earlier expense. Foreign-income recognition requires a source/location relationship with the income, receipt, or source entity in addition to a tax-outcome cue; an unrelated foreign shareholder mention or an overseas branch without income/profit/receipt is insufficient. Company-residency patterns require a company/corporate subject and a tax-residence concept. CPF/SRS/parent/cap relief, WHT royalty and GST input-tax phrases are mapped to their existing topics. No source maps, statutes, rates, URLs, or substantive rules were added.

Negative cases assert after reconciliation that accounting-only private expense, an SFRS(I) foreign-dividend accounting question, a generic overseas receipt accounting entry, and the SFRS(I) 6 accounting control do not acquire an IRAS workstream. A GST input-tax question about a director's private holiday does not create a corporate-tax workstream or residual. A separate private-travel journal plus explicit company-residency question keeps the residency route without inventing expense-deductibility topics; a corporate-tax question about a separate foreign dividend does not attach to that private expense. A local-subsidiary dividend remains local when a Thai shareholder appears in a separate sentence or comma-relative clause; neither foreign-sourced income nor Section 13 appears as a query, subject, filtered, or mapped candidate. Corporate tax treatment of rent paid for an overseas branch does not create a foreign-income topic. Foreign-tax-credit-only wording does not become a foreign-income topic. An ACRA resident-director question maps to ACRA without an IRAS route. Explicit Section 13 exemption wording remains a positive candidate case.

## Local provider and evidence result

The API-free diagnostic [iras-topic-source-path-v2.json](./iras-topic-source-path-v2.json) exercised the fixed nine-case v2 targeted set with `localOnly: true`, using the real resolver, source registry, default local provider and normal admission/verification pipeline. It disabled `fetch` and made zero model requests. Its 8 IRAS issues all mapped to at least one specific (non-routing-only) topic with an existing source-map pointer, and all 8 reached `retrievalAttempted`. Ten specific topic-to-map links were observed across those issues.

The local-only run found candidates and reached admission for 3/8 IRAS issues; 2/8 produced a verified rule claim. Five issue applications remained `UNRESOLVED`. The private-expense row had one verified claim but was still `INSUFFICIENT` because not every scope was covered. The WHT row also had one verified claim but remained `INSUFFICIENT`; the GST row had an admitted local candidate but no verified rule claim. CPF relief, foreign income and company residency had routing pointers but no local candidate in this no-fetch run.

This diagnostic establishes local mapping and provider-path entry. It does not show that the official pages were fetched, that a pointer yielded fresh page content, that every topic has locally admitted substantive support, or that a tax answer is complete. Source-map pointers are explicitly serialized as routing-only (`groundingEligible: false`); they are not counted as provider candidates, admitted records, verified claims, or source support. The uncovered local rows remain visible blockers for the IRAS release gate.
